How the prediction works CollapseExpand

1 · Your running sets the baseline

Running is about half of a HYROX race. In results data, run total alone explains roughly 82% of the variation in finish time, and VO₂max is the strongest single physiological correlate. Enter any recent races: 5K, 10K, half marathon, marathon. Each is converted with the Riegel formula and blended. 10K and half marathon count most because they match a HYROX effort (60–120 min near threshold), the 5K a little less, the marathon least.

That gives your HYROX kilometre pace. Mid-pack athletes run their laps about 20% slower than 5K pace; strong hybrid athletes 12–15%. The "~25–30%" often quoted for beginners is mostly fitness, not inexperience: less running volume, less durability and weaker muscular endurance. So the lap factor responds to:

  • Weekly running distance: more volume means less compromised running and less fade, with diminishing returns (about 2% better at 70 km / 45 mi a week, capped near 3%).
  • Endurance: a short plus a long race show how well you hold pace. Durable runners fade less.
  • First race: a small pacing allowance (about 1.5%) for everyone, plus an "unfamiliar with running on tired legs" part of up to 3.5% that shrinks with running volume, durable race times and regular gym/HYROX training.
  • Pro loads add a little; racing experience beyond a first race helps a little.

The eight runs follow the real pacing shape: Run 1 fastest, the runs after the sled push and lunges slowest, and Run 8 includes the finish straight. Elites and high-volume runners pace more evenly.

2 · Stations start from real race medians

Station, run and Roxzone baselines are median splits of about 15,000 Open results from results.hyrox.com, grouped by finish band. The app takes what athletes who run like you typically post, then adjusts each station for you:

  • SkiErg / Row: your fresh 1000 m time × 1.13 (in the race you hold about 85–90% of time-trial pace). 500 m, 2 km and 5 km times are converted. Taller athletes are slightly faster.
  • Sled Push / Pull: leg or pulling capacity (bodyweight + squat or deadlift) against a typical athlete, plus grip for the pull and a technique rating.
  • Burpee Broad Jumps: conditioning plus bodyweight. Taller athletes need fewer jumps.
  • Farmers Carry: deadlift and grip. Sandbag Lunges: load relative to squat, and stride length (height).
  • Wall Balls: your max unbroken set against the typical set for your level (about 100 for sub-60, 40 at 80–90 min, 20 at 120+).
  • Body fat: when your lifts are unknown, strength is estimated from lean mass, so a lean athlete is assumed stronger than a typical one of the same bodyweight. Measured lifts ignore it. VO₂max still counts a little when you have race times (races can be old or not all-out): up to ±1.5% on your running for a watch estimate, ±4% for a lab test.
  • Other training hours (gym, HYROX classes): up to ±3% on stations, never on the runs. Age 50+: a small recovery allowance, capped at 4%.
  • Pro and mixed loads: scaled with elasticities fitted to Pro vs Open splits at the same run pace.
  • Roxzone: typically 6–9% of race time. Your transition self-rating sets it; without one, first-timers get about 15% extra.
  • Guard rails: combined personal adjustments stay within 0.65–1.5× the typical split, and nothing goes below world-class floors.

3 · Missing data? Every ability has fallbacks

You can leave any field blank. The Quick view shows only the 10 inputs that move the prediction most (race time, experience, strength ratings, bodyweight, weekly running, wall balls, age, sex); Detailed shows the rest. Each ability uses the best information available, in this order:

  • Running: any race times (blended) › VO₂max (lab or watch, via Daniels VDOT) › self-rating (Weak ≈ 32:00 … Elite ≈ 17:30 5K for men).
  • Ergs: 1000 m › 500 m, 2000 m or 5000 m (Paul's law: +5 s/500 m per doubling) › the other erg ± the typical SkiErg-vs-row gap › self-rating.
  • Leg strength: back squat (a max or your usual working set; see below) › front squat ÷ 0.85 › deadlift × 0.8 › trap-bar or Romanian deadlift › leg press or bench (rough) › self-rating mapped to strength standards for recreational HYROX athletes (squat: Weak 0.6× … Solid 1.25× … Elite 2× bodyweight for men; deadlift: Solid 1.5×).
  • Pulling strength: deadlift › trap bar ÷ 1.08 › Romanian deadlift ÷ 0.75 › squat ÷ 0.8 › bench (rough) › self-rating.
  • Grip: farmers carry test › dead hang and/or pull-ups › self-rating.
  • Burpees: 80 m BBJ test › burpees in 1 minute › self-rating.
  • Wall balls: 100 reps for time › max unbroken › your usual set size for 100 reps (sets are about 60% of your max unbroken, so sets of 20 ≈ 33 unbroken) › self-rating.
  • Sleds and lunges: fresh station test × race-fatigue factor › strength and bodyweight model.

No max test needed. Most people never test a one-rep max, so a lift can be your usual working set: the weight, the reps, and how hard it felt (to failure, 1–2 reps left, 3–4 left or 5+ left). The reps you had left are added to the reps you did (reps in reserve), and the Epley formula turns that into an estimated max: 60 kg / 132 lb × 10 with 1–2 reps left ≈ 83 kg / 183 lb. Estimates get rougher above about 10 reps to failure and are capped at 15.

Self-ratings move each station by how much that station varies in real results: wall balls, lunges, burpee broad jumps and transitions spread widely, ergs barely. Every self-rating has a concrete description for each level. "Not sure" assumes a typical athlete at your running level: it never skews the result, it only widens the confidence range. The confidence panel shows what each estimate is based on and which single input would narrow your range most.

4 · Doubles & relay

Doubles: partners run together at the slower partner's pace, though less compromised because each does only part of the stations. Splitting a station lets you work harder. The model is calibrated so an even split matches observed doubles-to-singles ratios (sleds about 0.55–0.6×, wall balls about 0.66×, ergs about 0.9×). Elite pairs gain a little less. Every station starts at 50/50; you can move it between 20% and 80%, or tap Suggest a split for a practical plan (30–70%, in 5% steps) that gives each partner more of what they're faster at, e.g. a much taller partner takes more of the lunges and burpee broad jumps. You move through the Roxzone together, so it sits between your two singles Roxzone times, weighted towards the slower one, minus a little for resting while your partner works. Because you run every kilometre together, the slower runner sets the pace, which is why a strong but slower-running partner helps less than you might expect. Relay: each athlete runs 2 × 1 km and does 2 stations on fresh legs. The fastest order is chosen automatically. Insights also gives doubles hand-over tips: how often to switch on each station (e.g. SkiErg every 100–250 m, row every 250 m, sleds every length, wall balls every 10–15 reps) and how much each of you does.

5 · Insights & Simulator

Insights compares your runs, each station and the Roxzone with athletes like you: the same sex, age, height, weight, body fat, HYROX experience and other training, with typical fitness for that profile. They don't share your race times: your running, strength and station abilities are what's compared, so every gap is something training can change. Fitter runners are faster on the stations too, so your race times count there as well. Tap a row to see why it differs: your fitness (race times) is measured first, then each strength and station input at your fitness level, and the reasons add up to the gap; strength reasons show your estimated max next to the one assumed for athletes like you. Your build and background lists what bodyweight, body fat, height, age, first race, running volume and other training do to your finish time, compared with an average athlete who has your race times. Realistic gains are sized from training studies for your level and age (e.g. about 1–2% on a well-trained runner's 5K in 8–12 weeks, less over 50). They are offered only for things you entered or rated. Unknowns show as worth measuring, with how much they could swing your time; the app never invents a number for you. The Simulator lets you drag any split to see its effect on your finish and field position.

Accuracy

The likely range under the big clock shows the low and high end of where you'll probably finish. Its width comes from how much is measured versus assumed: measured inputs narrow it, "Not sure" widens it. From a single race time alone, expect roughly ±10%. Each benchmark narrows the range, and a previous HYROX result narrows it most. Venues differ in sled surfaces and Roxzone length, so treat the result as a pacing plan, not a promise. The field position is approximate and based on historical finish distributions, both overall and within your HYROX 5-year age group (e.g. Men 50–54), since older groups finish later on average.
